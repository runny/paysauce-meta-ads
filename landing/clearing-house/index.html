What the clearing house did, and why it went

Every employee can be in a different super fund. The Small Business Superannuation Clearing House let you make one payment to the ATO, and the ATO split it up and sent the right amount to each fund. It was free, and for a small employer it removed the fiddliest recurring job in payroll.

It was built for a world where super was paid four times a year. That world ended on the same day it did.

Until 30 June 2026

Super, four times a year

One payment to the ATO each quarter, free, covering every employee. Registrations for the service had already closed to new users in October 2025.

From 1 July 2026

Super, every pay run

Payday Super commenced. Super is now paid at the same time as wages, and contributions need to reach the fund within seven days. The clearing house could not work that way, so it was retired rather than rebuilt.

Two changes landed in the same month

On their own, each one is an annoyance. Together they turn a quarterly chore into a job you now do every single time you pay someone, without the free tool that used to make it simple.

What your options actually are

The ATO points former users in three directions. They are not equally good, and which one suits you depends mostly on how much you want to keep doing by hand.

Your super fund's clearing house

Most funds run one. Usually free. The catch is that it sits outside your payroll, so you are still moving numbers between two systems every pay run, and doing it far more often than before.

A commercial clearing house

Does the same distribution job the ATO used to. Often batch-based and manual, and usually another subscription on top of whatever you already pay for payroll.

Payroll that pays super itself

The super is calculated and sent as part of running the pay, so there is no separate step to remember and nothing to reconcile between systems. This is what we do.
